TITLE: The self-fulfilling prophecy
![Self-fullfilling-prophecy-1.png](/images/blog/Self-fullfilling-prophecy-1.png)

## Agile ways of working do not work!

- Agile working slows things down instead of delivering the promised increase in productivity and quality!
- With Scrum you have to carry out all disciplines every sprint! This takes more time!
- All the new meetings take up too much time! There is no room left for production!
- Since we started with agile, the hours per function point have increased!

These are just a few of the remarks often heard when starting an agile initiative. And especially when management interprets the first results of implementing agile ways of working in this way, it will become a self-fulfilling prophecy: introducing agile ways of working does not work!

However: agile initiatives require an investment! The ROI is a J curve!

![Text box: -](/images/blog/J-curve.png)


Causes of this (temporary) decrease in productivity (required investment):

- Dual way of governing;
- New working formats on top of existing meeting structures;
- Dual way of estimating and planning;
- Maturity of teams (Forming / Storming / Norming / Performing);
- Focus on the efficiency of individual employees, teams or even departments (sub-optimization) instead of focus on the effectiveness of the entire IT life cycle (optimization);
- The structure of the organization has not yet been adapted to the new process, resulting in people and teams having to answer to multiple managers, which creates conflicts of interest;
- Learning curves;
- Creating room for a sustainable pace (for example room for teams for education, innovation, reducing technical debt and continuous improvement);
- Relocating or retraining redundant staff;
- Changing the balance in staff from 3:1 to 1:3 in terms of coordination/management versus developers.

## What is needed to shorten the investment period as much as possible:

- Take the success criteria into account (see later)
- Management has to go through the necessary paradigm shifts (see my blog on '[Paradigm shifts in scaling agile](/nl/blog/paradigma-verschuivingen-bij-scaling-agile/)').
- The more and the faster the old paradigms can be abandoned, the more and the faster the benefit.

In the 2 graphs below we see this J-curve from an actual real-life situation. In this organization function points were already being measured before the agile transition started.

The solid green line is the moving average of the productivity, expressed in euros per function point and days per function point respectively, in the classic way of working.

The solid red line is the moving average of the productivity, expressed in euros per function point and days per function point respectively, in the agile way of working.

![Text box: Here is a real example of this pattern:: ________ = Moving average traditional ________ = Moving average Agile](/images/blog/Functiepunten.png)


You can clearly see the J-curve upside down here. Euros and days per function point first rise as a result of starting an agile transition. After about three quarters they dive below the classic level.

## The risk of getting stuck below the zero line of the J-curve

If management does not commit to their role as 'Lean Agile Leader' and is not aware of the redundancy and learning curves mentioned above, the organization runs the risk that productivity stays below the zero line of the J-curve.

This means management has to actively lead the transition. They are at the helm and should eliminate the hybrid situations mentioned, introduced when starting the agile initiative, as quickly as possible.

Here are a number of possible causes from practice why this often does not happen:

- The power of management threatens to be reduced (we want autonomous self-organizing teams, and decisions as low as possible in the organization);
- Management has been used to governing IT the same way for 60 years, and finds it hard to step out of that paradigm, just as IT embraced waterfall working for 60 years;
- Loss of control. People are used to controlling local KPIs, usually financial ones, but now we are going to steer on realized business value and productivity, what is that?;
- People cannot let go of the existing structure and hierarchy of the organization. This is often complicated by implicit self-created rules, or works councils;
- People are unable to create room in budgets and timelines / release dates to adapt the organization to the new way of working (for example, teams are still being 'chased' to meet the deadlines of pre-planned release dates at least a year ahead);
- People are not willing to invest in good training and coaching, and as a result the teams practise SINO (Scrum In Name Only);
- People are not willing to invest in tooling for automated deployment, automated testing and support of the agile processes.

As you can see, many things can lead to organizations getting stuck below the zero line of the J-curve. So good management training and coaching is needed just as badly as training and coaching for the teams!

If management (and I mean executive and middle management) does not allow themselves this, for whatever reason, the 'self-fulfilling prophecy' will come true!

NB: My 5 success criteria:

1.  Management commitment
2.  Agile change management with a multidisciplinary management team that itself follows the Scrum way of working for the organizational change
3.  Just-in-time training of all employees including management
4.  Intensive coaching of all teams until they have reached a certain level of maturity
5.  Measure both the change and the operation in a fixed rhythm and start on day 1 with baseline measurements. Create visual 'information radiators' (Obeya rooms)
