## **Introduction**

Agile working is everywhere. Both in business and in government, at least the IT departments are busy with (implementing) agile ways of working and mindset. Or at least attempts to do so. One company succeeds somewhat better than the other.

But what is so typical now: the governance of the companies or governments is still based on a way of governing that dates from the ice age.

And what companies and governments do not realize, or do not realize sufficiently, is that they introduce a dual way of governing the moment agile working is introduced. Even if it is only Scrum at team level. You introduce the so-called ‘PULL’ mechanism, while classic project governance works with a ‘PUSH’ mechanism. These ways of governing clash with each other. This is partly the reason why agile scaling frameworks have emerged that try to align a company's way of governing with this ‘PULL’ mechanism.

But even when applying scaling frameworks, companies and governments continue to cling rigidly to the classic way of governing, and thereby keep two ways of governing in place. This means the benefits of agile governance and working can only be partly achieved, or not at all.

## **Governance in the chain**

More and more I see that the cause of this is that an organization is also directed in a classic way from the outside. Because of this, boards of directors also feel compelled to direct their company in a classic way. This is strange, because the IT of the directing companies or governments switched to an agile way of working long ago, and so did the IT departments of the directed companies or governments!

## **The differences**

How nice would it be if the governance of the entire chain were aligned?? The fact that executive boards and directors of organizations do not see this break in the chain I find very serious. But is that conscious? Or unconscious?

Toyota decided back then to break with suppliers if they did not pursue the same quality and did not adopt continuous improvement as a driver. So the examples do exist. But Toyota's number 1 principle is: ‘Long-term thinking over short-term thinking’. And Western business seems not, or hardly, to adopt that.

## **A spanner in the works**

The chain acts in the same way as when fixed Scrum teams are introduced in an organization. People think they are getting the holy grail, but keep directing the teams in a classic way and holding them accountable for results, without actively working on making those teams more mature so that they achieve those results for you. This also applies at organization and chain level. If, as management, you do not set the boundaries stating that you want to steer on organizational development (read: making your organization more mature and more self-learning), then it will keep clashing in your organization, resulting in enormous overhead, coordination burden and always running behind the facts.

In practice, start looking at the departments or employees that direct the next organization (or organizational unit) in the chain. These can be policy departments, CIO offices, or procurement departments. These are the small blocks I have drawn in figures 1 and 2. As long as those employees are not given the assignment to direct the next organization in the chain in a lean agile way, as indicated in the table of differences, the spanner in the works will persist. Training and coaching those employees can help, but as long as they are not given the right boundaries from above, it is still not going to happen.

I challenge public administration and business scientists, and directors of organizations, to investigate how they can heal the break in the chain of governance that started with the introduction of agile working at team, organization and chain level.

Is it possible? Are there things I am completely overlooking? Do I see everything too simply and one-sidedly? That will certainly be the case; governments and companies have to comply with all kinds of legislation and rules. Still, I wonder…..  

The moment someone says: ‘That is not possible, because that is not how it works here’, or: ‘This has been imposed from above, we cannot change that’, I think: that sounds like a limiting belief. People make up rules and legislation themselves. So people can also change them, if only they want to!
