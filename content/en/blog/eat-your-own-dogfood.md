Implement Agile in an Agile way!

![dogfood](/images/blog/dogfood.jpg)

One of my principles is: ‘Apply an agile way of working when implementing large scale agile’. “That’s logical ain’t it?” will most agilists think. But try to sell this when doing big transformations in traditional governed ‘blue’ organizations.

It still is desirable to apply an agile change strategy as soon as you can sell it.

**Advancing insight**

As with software development and maintenance, a change process is continuously challenged by impediments and advancing insight. This makes an agile approach ideal for implementing an agile way of working in an organization. This would include:

* Management acts as a Scrum team;
* Management sets their agile ambition;
* Management creates a roadmap with sub goals to reach the ambition
* A core change team refines and prioritizes a change backlog derived from the roadmap;
* The core change team also acts as a Scrum team, running 4 to 6 weekly change sprints, including refinement, planning, review and retrospective sessions;
* The core change team demo’s the changes achieved to Management.  
  All of this requires a good Scrum Mastership, a management which is well aware of what agile entails and disciplined agile requirements management of the change.

**Bumps on the road and resistance**

As an agile process improver it should be easy to apply the agile principles in your own work. That is not the biggest hurdle. The organizations I encountered where mostly classically oriented and wanted to make the transition to an agile way of working. That’s a big paradigm shift as we know. These organizations are in fact used to implement changes through major programs and still want to see at least a bulky program plan, phases, deliverables, and so on. The same dilemma when coaching classically oriented project teams as a coach (“First a project plan and a schedule!”).

So it takes a lot of growing awareness, build customer relationships, advocacy, consultancy, workshops, etc. with a management team (whose agenda is already tight) to get them to move into a Scrum rhythm. Again you get the argument frequently heard when coaching engineering teams: “Yes, but we already have so many meetings, this comes on top of that!”.

Usually you’re not able to implement this kind of change process from day one. Often management first waits for the first success before they dare to make the step and commit to this. Quite often the Product Owner role for the change is first allocated to a single manager. But an enterprise-wide implementation impacts organization boundaries and every item on the management agenda! Think of things like culture, structure, architecture, HR, finance / governance.

**Benefits and experiences**

In my experience, the agile approach to change delivers a lot of advantages. Management will learn what an agile process implies, and thereby will lead by example. In this way they will also constitute a forum in which impediments to the change can be actively solved.

In a large transformation, I have taken the agile change approach a step further. Here I applied the principle of change features (the roadmap goals) being split into change stories for the change. Management defines and prioritizes change features on the roadmap, provide acceptance criteria and place them on a change backlog. The core change team pulls the change features and split them into change stories that can be implemented in one change sprint.

In the demo by the core change team impediments arise that need to be resolved. In this way, management of expectations is also assured. After all, senior management receives feedback on the successfully implemented change stories and the stories that still require help to complete successful implementation.

After several successful agile implementations in big organizations like this my slogan is: Implementing Large Scale Agile? Eat your own dog food!
