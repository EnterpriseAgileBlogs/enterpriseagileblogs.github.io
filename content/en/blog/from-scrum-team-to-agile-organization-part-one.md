**Introduction**

In reaction to the success of my Dutch blog series, I decided to translate this blog series to English.

In my work as a management coach, trainer and consultant in the field of lean and agile frameworks, I encounter many companies and IT departments. Through my extensive experience in large-scale implementations of agile frameworks I’ve discovered a pattern, or maturity levels if you will, that explain the degree of efficiency of these implementations.  
Types of companies I encountered are:

* Publishers
* Telcos
* Airlines
* Governmental institutions and agencies
* Financial institutions
* Insurers
* Technical product suppliers

Quite often huge return on investments are promised when implementing agile frameworks. Better quality, faster time to market, lower cost. The extent of the efficiency delivered depends on the quality of the implementation, and the extent to which the organization wants to evolve into an entire agile organization.  
The pattern that I observe in organizations I encounter is as follows, and it says something about the degree of Agile maturity of the organization :  
1. Working iteratively within (temporary) projects in a matrix organization  
2. Working in virtual product teams, assembled from functional oriented departments  
3. Working in physical product teams , possibly supplemented by virtual Ops  
4. DevOps within IT  
5 . Company-wide product or domain teams  
In a series of five blogs I will explain the advantages and disadvantages of these agile maturity steps.

**The Scrum approach to date is often initiated by IT**  
Agile is here to stay. With the exponentially rapid changing economy, the demand for a faster Time to Market is getting bigger and bigger, and matrix organizations nurture the growth of technical debt in IT landscapes to an extent that is irreversible.  
In the recent years I see many Agile / Scrum initiatives starting ‘bottom-up’, or ‘top-down’ in the IT department for various reasons . Or a team suggested it themselves, or management decides to, because they had been ordered to cut costs. Currently I see an enormous sense of urgency in organisations that cannot keep up anymore with the business demand, due to the reasons mentioned. 40 years of working in the same way in IT has to change drastically!!

**IT departments are typically organized in a matrix organization**  
In the Netherlands, most IT departments I encounter are organized as a matrix organization. They are organized in functional departments like project management, requirements management, testing and engineering, from which temporary project teams are staffed. We also see that Development and Operations departments are often separated, and mirrored. And I think it will be likewise in much foreign classical oriented organizations.

**Agile maturity step 1 : Working iteratively within (temporary) projects in a matrix organization**  
Projects in Development departments are staffed over and over again from the various functional oriented departments. This is the reason why Scrum in the beginning is mostly used in a project-oriented setting.

![Step 1](/images/blog/step-1.jpg)
  
This may bring advantages already, but not the advantages that are often promised. In Agile / Scrum we are talking about Product Owners and a Product Backlog. These are product -oriented business assets that require a Business Product oriented IT organization (assuming the client organization is already fairly Business Product oriented). If this is the case, you will see the return on investment of implementing an agile framework increase heavily. In addition, an agile approach is in conflict with the fixed scope that is often incorporated in project plans, and signed and approved in blood.

**Advantages and disadvantages of agile maturity step 1:**  
**Advantages:**

1. Iterative and risk-driven way of working delivers decrease in cost and time, although limited
2. Working with a backlog of business requirements results in an improved degree of acceptance (in IT, we are not used to working with requirements, we only think in solutions and design)
3. Early and frequent testing and demonstration brings better quality software and also contributes to lower costs , cycle time, and improved degree of acceptance
4. Transparency, by working as a Product Owner / representative of the business with a Product Backlog as a means to discuss about scoping the project also provides improved degree of acceptance

**Disadvantages:**

1. Management has a tendency to see agile / Scrum as just another development methodology, while it goes much further than that, and implicates management and governance to change also
2. Team members on temporary project teams are often working on multiple projects simultaneously , thereby having poor focus and much loss of time spent on focus shifting
3. Working with fixed scope and a classical governance model provides tension and conflicts with agile principles
4. Changing the staffing of teams for every project prevents team members to acquire domain knowledge
5. Changing the staffing of teams for every project prevents teams to build on predictability in terms of velocity and story points per sprint / product release
6. The traditional way of planning and budgeting is maintained, but takes a lot of overhead that could be heavily reduced when (fixed) teams are enabled to get predictable in an agile way
7. The attention of customer representatives often has to go to multiple projects simultaneously
8. Lack of predictability and productivity improvement, as a result of bullets 4, 5 and 6, often make management decide for cancellation of an agile initiative

Many organizations I work with are in this phase or step in their agile maturity. It may be a choice of management to not go beyond step 1 with their agile ambition. They should then have to realize that the return on investment will be sub optimal, as is this interpretation of an agile way of working.  
**In terms of percentage of return on investment, this step gets a 10%.**

In Part 2 of this series, I ‘m going to talk about agile maturity step 2: Working in (fixed) virtual product teams, assembled from functional oriented departments.

Note: When I am talking about agile maturity in this context, I talk about the organizational structure supporting the agile way of working. Many agile purists will comment that agile maturity is all about self organizing, empowerment, continuous improvement, shared code ownership, facilitating teams and so on, and I agree totally. But in this series I am taking a more organizational look at things.
