**My statement is: Scrum is the same thing as the Iterative development practice from Unified Process, but with different names.**

I am not going into what existed first, but I want to emphasize that many things are the same, and some things are different between Scrum and the Iterative Development Practice from Unified Process.

Here is my list of synonyms:

| Unified Process | Scrum |
|---|---|
| Iteration | Sprint |
| Iteration Plan | Sprint Plan |
| Iteration Assessment | Retrospective |
| Progress Meeting | Daily Stand-up |
| Iteration Lead | Scrum Master |
| Business Representative | Product Owner |
| Product Backlog | Product Backlog |
| Iteration Plan | Sprint Backlog |

The differences are that in Scrum people are encouraged to do visual planning and tracking, against documenting things in Unified Process.

However Unified Process also tells you to minimize the documentation needed using the development case, still, people experience the visualization of plans and tracking progress as very efficient and positive. I tend to feel comfortable with a minimum set of project documentation giving you an audit trail of what happened, every Iteration, for organizational metrics purposes.

**So, what is my point?**

My point is that the iterative development practice can be used as easy as scrum to implement an iterative way of working.

And the Iterative Development Practice, is just one of the 12 practices in OpenUP at this moment.

Companies should not stare blindly at just the buzz word Scrum, but should be aware that Scrum just covers the Project Management Discipline (bold to say, since Scrum does not know the role of Project Manager..;-)).

Scrum says nothing about how to deal with releases, changes, testing, integration, as where Unified Process does to a certain extent.

I am not advocating Unified Process here, but merely stating that organizations implementing Scrum will eventually feel the need to also do something with these other practices. And also they will feel the need to adjust their existing processes to support the agile way of working. Like financial reporting, Governance, Tollgate rules, Release management, contracting suppliers, budgeting.

The latter is also needed when implementing Unified Process. Implementing any Agile method will have its effect on an organization, since it implies a paradigm shift.

So you could just as easy implement the Iterative Development Practice as you could Scrum. And you could even replace the Use Cases with User Stories, and do Daily Stand-ups, and plan and track visually.

It is not in the name, like Scrum is the Walhalla of Agile, it is in the perception that the organization has of Agile, it’s principles, and the methods that support the Agile principles.

So my advice to organizations that want to go agile:

Do not stare blindly at the buzz word Scrum. But be aware of the agile principles, the agile methods, and then select practices that you feel comfortable with to implement.

It is not necessary anymore these days to adapt complete methods. But you pick the things from it you can use in your organization, you mix and match, and start small. No Big Bang implementations, but use the oil spill effect in your organization.

Agile is not Scrum, but just a bunch of principles. And people, especially managers, should be aware of those principles, and willing to adapt those, before starting to implement any Agile method.
