Taking into account that my 5-part series was written in 2013, and focuses mostly on structure and governance, I felt I still wanted to write a closing blog.

Having now gained another 4 years of experience helping classically oriented organizations introduce agile ways of working, I see that leadership is the most important element for every organization on this journey. It prompted me to focus on helping executive and middle management when I became independent again this year.

If we look at the number 1 impediment to agile transitions in this graph from the 11th edition of VersionOne's 'State of Agile', you see that the culture of companies is usually the total opposite of the agile core values. Yet these companies also try to ride along with the trend, why? The main reason is simply having to survive in this period of exponential economic and technological change. But these classic organizations then have a long way to go.

It all starts with top management, who must understand which changes they need to initiate in their leadership, but also in their entire organization, in order to start the journey correctly. It is amazing how many managers still see the agile movement as 'another' system development methodology, or 'something' of IT.

When I finished my last assignment I received the book ‘Reinventing Organizations’ by Frederic Laloux from the client's management team. Very recognizable, and it offers a view of how organizations evolve, or should evolve, over time. Coincidentally, the organization types shown below fit the maturity levels for structure and governance as I described them in this series.

When I look at the figure, I recognize many organizations where I carried out assignments somewhere in the yellow or orange type. These organizations still have to go through quite a few paradigm shifts to become 'green', let alone 'teal'. Next week I will re-post my blog about the (at least) 40 paradigm shifts organizations have to go through on the journey to an agile organization.

Finally, I want to explain the figure I used for the entire series. Last year I was inspired by Jaap Peters at the annual conference of the Agile Consortium, and again at the Belgian equivalent. Jaap is a well-known speaker and advocates the transition from classic Anglo-Saxon oriented organizations to the Rhineland model. You could see the Rhineland model represented in the colour 'teal' in the figure above. Jaap states that Anglo-Saxon oriented organizations are still in the Middle Ages, thinking that employees are merely dumb workhorses who must be told what to do, and who must be controlled. This in contrast to a Rhineland organization, where employees are seen as the most important assets of the company, part of self-organizing teams, and management merely represents the facilitating 'back end' of the company. The title figure used for this series visualizes this tilt from functional silos to product silos.

I challenge every manager to become acquainted with the books of Frederic Laloux and Jaap Peters. There is more to agile transitions than most people suspect, as this series shows, among other things, while it only touches on a few areas of attention (structure and governance). And then we have not even talked about leadership, mindset, architecture, ownership, continuous improvement, staff departments, etc. If top management convinces itself that this is the challenge they face, then my work, and that of all other agile coaches, becomes much more effective!
