Agile production and maintenance of software is becoming more and more common. Many IT organizations nowadays are confronted with System Integrators offering to work agile and iterative.  Also the other way around, many IT organizations are demanding more and more from System Integrators to work iteratively and deliver working software every iteration.

So what does this imply for the IT demand organization? What changes can IT Demand Project Managers expect?

**Agile is becoming common and the only way to produce and maintain software in the modern and fast changing society.**

With the ever changing and faster moving society comes the need for businesses to adapt to the market more quickly. This means a faster time to market is demanded from IT organizations by the business organizations.

The average duration for a change request from first notification to production is two times the average duration of a release in your IT organization. With rigid release schedules with releases that span six months, this means a time to market of one year!

**Many organizations are in transition from classical waterfall to a more collaborative and iterative way of working.**

More and more IT demand as well as System Integrator organizations recognize the advantages of agile production and maintenance of software. Cost and duration savings up to 50% are not rare. And in the current crisis situation cost reduction is key, improved time to market is what the business wants, and improved quality a very pleasant side effect.

I encounter many organizations in transition to adapting Agile methods like RUP and Scrum. And one company is more successful than the other, and some of them are just starting up, and others have been transitioning for years already. It now seems an oil spill effect.

**What are the implications for an IT demand organization**

IT demand organizations are confronted with three paradigm shifts.

1. First of all there’s the paradigm shift from going from plan driven to value driven way of working and handling of project tolerances:

![](/images/blog/paradigm-shift.jpg)

2. The second paradigm shift is for the project managers. They will have to go from ‘Managing’ to ‘Directing’. This means they are used to handle fixed price projects as a black box, and managing many projects like that at the same time.  They are able to do this, because they only will have to check a report from the System Integrator regularly, like once a week. They are more of a demand manager and are managing by exception.

Directing means the Project Managers have to take control of the process themselves, and manage the iterations or sprints, and together with the business define the work packages to be assigned to the System Integrator every iteration or sprint. This takes a lot more discipline and time, but the Project manager also has to go into more detail of the process. By directing the process him selves, the whole content of the project becomes transparent, and a ‘glass box’ instead of a ‘black box’.

When Project Managers are used to the role of Demand, and managing by Exception, they might not like the micro management needed with working in iterations or sprints. That is why you see in many organizations Scrum master and Iteration lead roles are not performed by the Project manager. He still has a role though by managing all the environment of the projects. But  managing ten projects at the same time is history.

Thus a good HR policy when engaging these kind of changes is very important.

3. The third Paradigm shift is creating a chain top down from Business Requirements prioritized on Business Value, to translation into system requirements, to translation into small chunks of work (Use case Modules or user Stories) to be assigned to the System Integrator.

* The Business maintains a Business requirements backlog containing all Business Changes per product, prioritized on Business Value.
* The IT demand maintains a backlog with Use case Modules or user Stories from which Product releases can be defined with a fixed duration. from a backlog maintained by the IT demand.
* IT demand defines sets of Use Case Modules or User Stories to assign to the system Integrator per Iteration or sprint.
* The System Integrator, when working with offshore party, can chunk up the Modules or User Stories for assignment to the offshore party within the iteration or sprint.

This big change In the way projects are turned into more like product releases also requires a big change in governance. Allowing product teams to process requirements for a product, using up a yearly budget with a fixed team bandwidth is the most efficient way to leverage the agile way of working. In a separate blog post I will go more in detail about product teams and product releases.

**Conclusion**

When engaging in changing the way of working to Agile and iterative in a big organization with multiple demand supply chains, the whole organization is impacted. This is a huge cultural and organizational change, that has to be taken seriously by top management.

Hiring separate experts to coordinate the change throughout the organization and implementing step by step for me is a mandatory thing. Internal people, nor existing System Integrators will ever succeed in achieving this, because management also has to change.
