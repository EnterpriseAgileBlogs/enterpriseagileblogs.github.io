## **Observation**

I come across it frequently in my practice. Companies that say they want to become agile or nimble, but when push comes to shove, do not want to change. How come?

Many articles related to this subject have already been written, but I would still like to give my view on it.

I recently spoke with a transformation manager at a large telco. His assignment was to make his department agile. But when he drew the org chart to indicate where his department sat, I saw it right away: it was a functional department. As an example: the networks department. Of course you can introduce some agile working within a functional department, but if you want to become agile as a whole company, that is sub-optimal. You want to organize in value streams (see further on). Example of possible (main) value streams of a telco: Telephony, Internet, Television.

You cannot carry out a transformation to an agile organization (read: an organization that continuously adapts, changes and improves itself according to circumstances) as a big bang. But top management structurally lacks a picture of what an agile organization can look like. Let alone that there is a vision of how they want the organization to look in 2 years. Many articles have also been written about this, stating that top management has other things on its mind, but an agile transformation simply concerns a total and radical change of all business units, which must be looked at holistically. That therefore requires precisely the utmost attention of top management. I see all too often that people (often partly out of ignorance, but also fear of loss of control, fear of loss of position and power) refuse or fail to, for example, adapt the structure, or the governance of the organization. Whereas for me these two (besides the right mindset for employees and leadership, but I will come back to that later) are precisely the most important.

## **What is the solution?**

There is no super solution for this, but I can offer a few pointers that people can seriously think about before starting a journey towards an agile organization.

1.     ** Leadership**

a.      As top management, be willing to learn, so that you can make the right decisions yourself, and give your employees the right boundaries for a good transition

b.      After you have learned, run a vision workshop to determine, for the WHAT (your business vision), how you are going to fill in the HOW. What does our organization look like in 2 to 3 years, in terms of HR, budgeting, structure, governance? And then do not regard that as a new status quo, but implement changes iteratively too, and adjust your vision periodically based on experience gained.

c.      Focus much more on developing your organization, instead of steering on results; shift that balance bit by bit

d.      Give room for process improvement, product improvement and innovation, to be spent by teams themselves. Think of 20 to 25% of all capacity. A dairy farm also puts time into repairing the roof, innovating the milking machine, clearing away the waste, and so on. The feeling is then that you become 25% less productive, but the opposite is true. See also my blog: </en/blog/measuring-velocity-producing-milk-or-shoveling-shit/>

2.      **Structure**

a.      Run an enterprise-wide value stream workshop to find out what the possibilities are for adapting the structure to optimally support agile working. Involve portfolio managers, architects and the business.

b.      Choose one of those value streams as a pilot, to initiate a team of teams that is product (and therefore value) oriented.

c.      If successful (depending on many more factors), adapt the structure of your organization to the division into value streams, so that you can also introduce a new form of governance (Lean budgeting)

d.      That there is never one road to Rome, and that you can never organize an organization into unambiguous value streams, I explain in this blog: </en/blog/fractal-structure-patterns-when-scaling-agile-enterprise-wide/>

3.      **Governance**

a.      At the start of a transition you already undergo a paradigm shift: from fixed scope to fixed lead times and costs. Introducing Scrum already brings that with it. With that you already undermine existing ways of governing, such as the project matrix organization, which is based on fixed scope. So be willing to experiment in pilots with a new way of governing (Agile portfolio management)

b.       Be aware of the presence of the dual way of governing during the transition. This creates enormous tensions, and if it lasts too long it gives the feeling: “You see, that agile working does not work”. See my blog on this: [The self-fulfilling prophecy](/en/blog/the-self-fulfilling-prophecy/)

c.      Involve the CFO too, and experiment with new ways of budgeting (Lean budgeting, quarterly cycles instead of an annual cycle)

These are the tips I want to give from my experience. It is not the holy grail, but with the right support these are crucial steps to take and an increase in the chance of a successful transition.

## **Epilogue**

Obviously, pursuing these tips cannot be a top-down story from management. In my experience there must be both bottom-up and top-down action. Above all, gather ideas from the employees as well. Practice shows that that is where the best ideas lie for tackling many challenges.
